06 · single-stream speed

Per-token decode latency at batch 1

  • TP=2 costs nothing at batch 1 despite ~176 NCCL collectives per step: CUDA graphs hide the launch latency.
  • PP=2 is 1.75× slower: stages run one after another with a single request.
  • NVFP4 (weight-only FP4) decodes as fast as FP8: at batch 1 the GPU is reading weights, not computing.
07 · prefill

Why time-to-first-token is slow

  • Linear in prompt length, so it is not attention. The GPU is busy, so it is not CPU launch overhead.
  • Two thirds is the expert matmul running at ~17 % of peak: 512 experts of 1024×2688 each see ~176 rows per 4k prompt.
  • TP=2 does not help prefill (499 vs 535 ms): it halves each expert's width and makes the GEMMs smaller still.
  • Fix found: Humming MoE backend, −25 % TTFT at 4k and 8k with identical decode.

10 · predictions vs measurements

What the first-principles model got right and wrong

✘ wrong
Expected
TP=2 adds ~12 ms/token of all-reduce latency
Measured
TP=2 TPOT 10.3 ms vs 11.0 ms on 1 GPU — no penalty
Why
CUDA graphs hide launch latency; the 70 µs torch-level figure included it.
✘ wrong
Expected
PP=2 beats TP=2 (one hop vs 176 collectives)
Measured
In isolation PP=2 ≈ TP=2 (995 vs 1114 tok/s at 256 streams) with a better latency tail; not faster
Why
Communication is cheap under CUDA graphs either way. The first PP=2 run (½ of TP=2) was host contention, not pipelining.
✘ wrong
Expected
Experiments on disjoint GPUs are independent
Measured
PP=2 ran at 55 tok/s with NVFP4 jobs on GPU 0, 88 tok/s alone; throughput halved
Why
The pipeline-driving engine loop is host-sensitive. All shared-host numbers are lower bounds; TP=2 is being re-run alone.
✔ held
Expected
Three GPUs: more parallelism = more throughput
Measured
PP=3: 2215 tok/s at 256 streams (2× TP=2); 3 NVFP4 replicas: 1475 at 48 then memory-capped
Why
Layouts that replicate expert weights hit the Mamba-state ceiling; PP=3 and DP+EP keep 26–42 GiB of cache per GPU.
✔ held
Expected
NVFP4 on Hopper saves memory, not compute
Measured
Same decode speed as FP8; prefill 7–8k tok/s, MoE GEMM at 17 % of peak
Why
Weight-only Marlin dequant; experts of 1024×2688 are too small to run efficiently.
✔ held
Expected
Memory limits concurrency, not context
Measured
FP8 TP=2 failed to start: 1024 default seqs > 632 Mamba blocks; KV is 4 KB/token
Why
162 MiB of Mamba state per sequence vs 1 GiB of KV for a 128k context.
✔ held
Expected
fp16 Mamba state doubles concurrency for free
Measured
5.9× → 10.4× max concurrency; identical speed; GSM8K 92.4 vs 92.2 on all 1319 questions
Why
Also unchanged: ARC-Challenge 69.3 vs 69.3, IFEval within noise. Re-check only for 8k+ token generations.
✘ wrong
Expected
'12B active' = fast at any batch
Measured
FP8 TP=2 saturates at 1.1k tok/s on 2 GPUs at 190–250 W
Why
At ≥64 streams × top-22, every expert is hit each step: ~56 GB of weights per GPU per step.
✘ wrong
Expected
Adding a GPU via TP speeds up prefill
Measured
FP8 TP=2 4k TTFT 499 ms ≈ NVFP4 1-GPU 535 ms
Why
TP halves each expert's width (2688→1344) making tiny GEMMs even less efficient.
✔ held
Expected
Smaller prefill chunk smooths decode
Measured
8192→2048: p99 ITL 509→288 ms for −4 % throughput; 512 kills throughput
Why
The knob trades decode smoothness for prefill efficiency; 2048 is the sweet spot here.
✘ wrong
Expected
MoE kernel backend is a minor detail
Measured
Marlin→Humming: 4k TTFT −25 %, 16-stream throughput +16 %, decode unchanged
Why
The model card's Marlin workaround (for a crash never hit) cost 15 % at 4k.
✘ wrong
Expected
MTP speculative decoding is a free speedup
Measured
2.25× on greedy code (219 vs 97 tok/s); −35 to −55 % throughput on the random-token benchmark (13 % draft acceptance)
Why
Speculation pays only when output is predictable; the draft layer also costs 3 GiB of cache per GPU.
✘ wrong
Expected
Expert parallelism fixes prefill and the throughput plateau
Measured
TP=2+EP ≈ TP=2 everywhere; +5–10 % at 128 streams, collapses at 256; 4k TTFT 532 vs 499 ms
Why
The FP8 grouped GEMM is inefficient on these expert shapes however experts are split; EP adds all-to-all and load imbalance at high batch.
